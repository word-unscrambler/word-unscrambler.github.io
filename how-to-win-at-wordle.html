How to Win at Wordle
Every Single Day

Wordle has one puzzle per day, six guesses, and a single 5-letter target word. It sounds simple — but strategy separates consistent 3-guess solvers from players who regularly hit guess 6 or fail entirely. This guide covers the mathematics of optimal play, the best starting words, how to decode every color pattern, and advanced techniques for the hardest puzzles.

How Wordle Works: The Mechanics

Each Wordle puzzle has a single 5-letter answer. You get 6 guesses. After each guess, every tile turns one of three colors:

The goal of each guess is not just to get letters right — it's to maximize information gained. A gray result is just as valuable as a green: it eliminates a letter entirely, shrinking the possibility space. This information-theoretic perspective is the foundation of optimal Wordle strategy.

Advanced Strategy: Information Theory in Practice

The mathematical framework underlying optimal Wordle play is information theory, specifically Claude Shannon's concept of entropy. Each guess should maximally reduce the number of possible remaining answers. The key principles:

Never Waste Guesses Confirming Known Information

If you've confirmed a letter is in the word, don't reuse it in the same position on your next guess just to confirm it further. Every letter slot is valuable — use it to test a new, unconfirmed letter. The confirmed letter is already contributing to your knowledge base.

The Two-Guess Elimination Strategy

The optimal approach for non-hard-mode Wordle: spend guesses 1 and 2 purely on information gathering, regardless of any green results on guess 1. Two guesses testing 10 unique high-frequency letters leaves an average of just 2–8 possible answers for guess 3. From there, solving is usually trivial.

When to Switch from Elimination to Solving

With 3 or fewer remaining guesses, switch entirely to solving mode. No more elimination plays — every guess must be a valid candidate answer. This is where letter position knowledge becomes critical: you've confirmed 3–4 letters and their positions, and you need to construct a valid word around them.

Handling the "Hard Endgame" (_IGHT Pattern)

Some letter patterns produce many valid words: _IGHT (might, right, light, night, sight, fight, tight, wight), _ATCH (catch, hatch, latch, match, patch, watch), _OUND (bound, found, hound, mound, pound, round, sound, wound). When you find yourself with one of these patterns and 3 guesses left, systematically test the most likely first letters based on remaining letter frequency in the answer pool.

Pattern Recognition Shortcut

If your answer ends in -IGHT after guess 2, use guess 3 to test "MIGHT" — it covers M (likely), I (confirmed), G (confirmed), H (confirmed), T (confirmed) plus the M tests a probable first letter. If wrong, you still have LIGHT, NIGHT, RIGHT, SIGHT remaining with 3 guesses.

Hard Mode Strategy

Wordle's Hard Mode requires all revealed hints to be used in subsequent guesses: green letters must stay in position, yellow letters must appear somewhere in the word. This prevents the two-guess elimination strategy and forces you to work with confirmed letters from the start.

Hard Mode demands a different opening word — one with slightly lower entropy but better position coverage. STARE and RAISE perform better in Hard Mode than CRANE because their letter positions align with more common answer patterns. The opening word choice matters less, however, than this core Hard Mode principle: always test the ambiguous first letter when facing a vowel-consonant pattern with multiple possibilities. If the answer could be LIGHT, MIGHT, NIGHT, or SIGHT with one guess remaining, pick the one whose first letter seems most probable for a common word — and commit.

Build Your Wordle Vocabulary

Many Wordle failures happen not from poor strategy but from not knowing the word exists. Wordle draws from a curated list of common 5-letter words — uncommon words and proper nouns are excluded. The answer words lean toward everyday vocabulary. However, some less-common words do appear and will trip up players who haven't encountered them.

Common Wordle answer categories worth studying:

  • Body parts and anatomy: TIBIA, FEMUR, ANKLE, ELBOW, SCALP, SPINE, GROIN, CHEEK
  • Nature and animals: ALGAE, FINCH, HIPPO, HYENA, LARVA, LLAMA, SWAMP, FUNGI
  • Food and drink: CREPE, FUDGE, GUAVA, LEMON, OLIVE, PRUNE, SCONE, TAPAS
  • Common -ER words: BOXER, CAPER, DINER, GIVER, LONER, MISER, PAYER, TIGER, VOTER
  • Common -LY adverbs used as adjectives: BURLY, CURLY, EARLY, GIRLY, LOWLY, SURLY
  • Double-letter words (often harder): ABBEY, ADDED, ALOOF, EERIE, IGLOO, OTTER, VIVID, FUZZY
